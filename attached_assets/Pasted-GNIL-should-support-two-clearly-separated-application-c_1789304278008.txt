GNIL should support two clearly separated application contexts:

```text
1. GNIL Client App
   one deployment
   one client
   one business

2. GNIL Platform Control Plane
   internal software-owner administration
   may manage multiple client installations
```

The client must never experience the platform’s multi-business concepts.

Do NOT remove internal tenant database scoping.
Do NOT rewrite the core single-business runtime.
Do NOT expose platform tools to normal merchant/staff users.

## 1. Audit route/UI ownership

Classify all routes/pages/modules into:

```text
CLIENT_APP
PLATFORM_CONTROL_PLANE
PUBLIC
INTERNAL_INTEGRATION
```

Pay special attention to:

* tenants
* tenant detail
* governance
* franchise
* platform billing
* multi-business reporting
* provisioning
* platform emergency targeting
* co-op administration

## 2. Client app requirements

The client app must always resolve the sole active business automatically.

Normal users must never see:

```text
Tenant
Tenant ID
Select Business
Switch Business
Installations
Organizations
Tenant Operations
```

Client navigation should contain only business-operating features.

## 3. Platform control plane requirements

If multi-business tools are retained, place them behind a clearly separate platform-only boundary.

Requirements:

* `super_admin` / platform-admin only
* not linked from merchant/staff navigation
* distinct route namespace where practical, such as:

```text
/platform/*
```

* clear naming that this is internal platform administration
* no accidental exposure to merchant/front-desk/staff roles

Do not rename internal tenant IDs just for cosmetics.

## 4. Tenant/business administration

Move or isolate:

* Tenant Operations
* Tenant Detail
* platform business provisioning
* cross-business user assignment
* installation billing
* module provisioning reports
* multi-business health/status
* platform-wide broadcasts

into the control plane.

## 5. Franchise/multi-location review

Determine whether franchise functionality is actually part of the client product.

If the current product requirement is strictly one business / one location, move franchise administration to platform-only or remove it from client navigation.

Do not add a location selector to the client app.

## 6. Co-op distinction

Keep cross-business co-op participant IDs where required.

Differentiate:

```text
business A transacting with business B
```

from:

```text
user switching GNIL business context
```

The first may remain.
The second must not exist in the client app.

## 7. Authorization

Add/verify explicit platform-only guards for control-plane routes.

Normal roles such as:

```text
merchant
manager
staff
front desk
```

must receive 403 or have no route access to platform control-plane functions.

## 8. Frontend navigation tests

Verify:

### Merchant

No platform links.

### Staff

No platform links.

### Front desk

No platform links.

### Platform admin

Platform control-plane navigation may appear.

## 9. API separation

Normal client APIs should continue automatically resolving the app business.

Platform endpoints may explicitly reference business IDs because their purpose is administering multiple installations.

Make this distinction clear in code comments and API contracts.

## 10. Final report

Return:

### Client app routes

List major namespaces.

### Platform routes

List all retained multi-business namespaces.

### Authorization

Show platform-only roles.

### Navigation

Show what a merchant sees vs platform admin.

### Remaining explicit tenant IDs

Explain why each is platform or transactional rather than client business selection.

End state:

```text
Client:
"I log in and operate my business."

Platform owner:
"I can administer GNIL installations separately."
```
